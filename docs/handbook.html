What this system is

An ERP is one place where the whole business is written down, instead of a folder of spreadsheets that nobody else can open. When a timekeeper records hours on site, the accountant sees the cost on that job and the payroll clerk sees it on the payslip. When the storekeeper issues cable to the same job, its cost lands there too, and the manager sees whether the job is still inside the budget the estimator priced it at. Nobody re-types anything, and there is only ever one version of the truth.

This system is built for a UAE engineering and technical-services group. That shapes almost every screen: it knows about the FTA and VAT 201, about MOHRE and the WPS file your bank wants, about retention held back on a certificate, about the summer midday break, and about gratuity at the end of service. You will not find generic software wording where a UAE term belongs.

Four companies, one system

The group runs four legal companies, and each keeps its own books, its own employees and its own tax registration. You switch between them with the dropdown at the top-left of every screen. Nothing crosses between them by accident — a voucher posted in WBE can never touch WBTS.

Signing in

Open the address your administrator gave you, and enter your email and password. A few things behave deliberately:

  • After three wrong passwords from your computer, sign-in pauses there for fifteen minutes. Someone guessing from elsewhere does not lock you out; only a spread attack from many places pauses the account for everybody, and then every administrator is told. It is what stops somebody guessing their way in.
  • A wrong email and a wrong password get the same message on purpose, so the screen cannot be used to find out who works here.
  • A password needs at least ten characters. A short sentence you will remember is easiest — “Green tea at 7 on Friday” — and much harder to guess than a short word with symbols.
  • If an administrator has just set or reset your password, you are asked to choose your own before you can go any further. Nobody else should know your password, including them.
  • A sign-in lasts twelve hours. Sign out ends it on every device at once, so use it on a shared computer.
  • Signing in, signing out, and every failed attempt are written into the audit trail with the time and the address you came from.
Never share a loginEverything anybody does is recorded against the name they signed in with. Two people on one login means the record is wrong, and there is no way to untangle it afterwards.

Changing your own password

Open the account menu at the top-right, then My Account & Password. Changing it signs you out of every other device automatically — which is exactly what you want if you think somebody else has been using it.

Finding your way around

Three things are on every screen, always in the same place.

The left menu

Down the left-hand side

Everything you are allowed to open, grouped by area. Click a group heading to collapse it. The arrow at the top shrinks the whole menu to icons when you want more room.

If a menu item you expected is not there, you have not lost it — your role does not include it. See Users and access.

The company switcher

Top-left, above the page

Changes which company's records you are looking at. It follows you as you move between screens, so you will not find yourself reading the wrong company's figures after a few clicks.

Screens that bring every company together — People, Approvals, the Dashboard and each module's dashboard, Overtime, Man-hours, Compliance & Expiry, Separation and HR Tasks — have their own Company filter above the list. It starts on All my companies; pick one to see only that company. You only ever see the companies you belong to.

If you belong to one company only, the filter shows that company's name instead of a list — there is nothing to choose, but the screen still says whose records you are reading. To work in another company, ask an administrator to add it to your user.

The tabs across the top

Inside Finance, HR, CRM & Estimation, and Inventory & SCM

Each module keeps its screens as tabs along the top. The first tab is always Dashboard — see below. Finance and HR hold a lot of screens, so their tabs come in two levels. The top row is the area — Accounts, Entry, Registers, Job Costing, Reports, Tax, Setup. When the area you opened has more than one screen in it, a smaller second row appears underneath listing them.

At the far right of that row, All reports opens the full list of reports across the whole system.

A dashboard for each module

Finance, HR, CRM & Estimation, and Inventory & SCM — the first tab

Clicking a module in the menu opens its dashboard: what needs someone today, and how that part of the business is doing. The main Dashboard at the top of the menu is the same idea across every module at once.

  • Finance — income and profit this year, with the last six months side by side; cash in the bank; what customers owe and what we owe suppliers; cheques falling due in the next 30 days; drafts not yet issued; retention; VAT this quarter.
  • HR — who is present today and who is not yet marked; leave waiting for a decision; visas, Emirates IDs, labour cards and passports expiring within 60 days; the latest payroll runs; overtime and hours booked this month; certificates, vacancies and settlements. No salary appears on it.
  • Stores (Inventory & SCM) — what the stock is worth and in which store; items at or below their reorder level; material requests waiting to be ordered; purchase orders still to arrive, and late ones; deliveries waiting for inspection; instruments out of calibration.
  • Sales (CRM & Estimation) — the open pipeline by stage and what it is likely to be worth; what has been won this year and how often; quotations expiring in the next 14 days, awaiting approval, or accepted.

Every figure is a link to the screen it comes from, and the dashboard only shows figures from screens your role can open. So two people opening the same dashboard can see different panels: a site engineer sees stock and requests but not purchase orders or supplier prices; a timekeeper sees only today's muster. A coloured bar along the top of a figure marks what needs attention — gold for soon, red for overdue or expired, green for all well.

The quickest way to learn a screenClick the ? in the top bar. It shows help for the exact page you are looking at, in the same plain English as this handbook.

Small things worth knowing

  • Day and night mode. The toggle in the top bar. Your choice is remembered on that device.
  • Dates are British format throughout — 31/12/2026 is the last day of December, never the twelfth of the month.
  • Printing. Any report prints properly from the screen: the menus and buttons drop away and the company letterhead appears at the top. Documents that go to customers, suppliers or staff — quotations, orders, invoices, payslips, store notes — have their own Print button that produces a PDF. See Printed documents.
  • Getting data out. Most screens have an Export button that gives you a spreadsheet file.

Setting up

The first week, in order

This is the one genuinely sequential part of the system: each step needs the one before it. Work down the list and nothing will refuse you halfway through.

  1. Complete each company's details Companies → the pencil icon. Fill in the VAT registration number (TRN), and the address split into line, city and emirate. No invoice can be issued without the TRN — a tax invoice is not valid without it — and the emirate travels as its own field on an electronic invoice.
  2. Check the chart of accounts Finance → Accounts. A standard UAE contracting chart is already there. Mark every bank and petty cash account with Control account → Cash or bank: that is what the Cash Flow Forecast starts from, and a missed one makes the forecast start short.
  3. Enter opening balances If you are moving from another system, put each account's closing balance in as its opening balance, and set the date those balances are stated as at. Do this before posting anything new.
  4. Add customers and suppliers Finance → Registers → Parties. Record the TRN and the payment terms in days — the terms decide every due date the system calculates afterwards, so you never type one.
  5. Set the finance settings Finance → Setup. The VAT rate, the corporate tax band, when a cheque goes stale, and payday — the day of the month wages are paid, which the cash flow forecast turns on.
  6. Write down your HR policy HR → HR Policy. Leave, probation, notice, overtime rates, sick pay, gratuity, the air ticket. The system will not let you set anything below what UAE law requires, and it tells you which article says so.
  7. Add your people HR → People → Employees. Then their documents — visa, Emirates ID, MOL Person ID, passport — with the expiry dates, so the Compliance report can warn you before anything lapses.
  8. Set up stores and items Inventory & SCM → Stores, then Items. Each store with its kind and, where it is divided, its bins; each item with its unit, and whether it needs QA/QC inspection. Then record the stock already on the shelves, store by store.
  9. Set the letterhead and email Settings → Printed Documents for each company: logo or letterhead artwork, contact details and standard terms. Then Settings → Email, so quotations can be sent from the system. Print the sample and send a test before anything real goes out.
  10. Give people access Users, then Access Control. Grant each role only the screens that role needs. See Users and access.
Do not skip step oneEverything to do with invoicing is blocked until each company has its TRN. The system will tell you, but it is a frustrating way to find out on a Thursday afternoon.
Finance

Money, in plain words

You do not need to understand double-entry bookkeeping to use this system, but four words appear everywhere and are worth two minutes.

Voucher
One transaction, written down. Every entry in the accounts is a voucher, and each gets a number that is never reused — WBE/SI/26-26/0003 means WB Engineering, sales invoice, financial year 26–26, the third one.
Debit and credit
Two sides of the same transaction. Every voucher has to have both, and they have to be equal. The system checks this and will not let an unbalanced voucher through, so you cannot create a mess by accident.
Account
A named pot that money moves in and out of — Cash at Bank, Accounts Receivable, Salaries & Wages. The full list is your chart of accounts.
Posting
Committing a transaction to the books. Before it is posted, nothing has happened. After it is posted, it is part of the record and can be reversed but not quietly edited.

Invoicing customers

A tax invoice is a legal document, not a letter. UAE law says what has to be on the face of it, and the system will not let you issue one that is missing anything. That is why there are two stages.

Stage one: the draft

Finance → Entry → Invoices → New invoice

Choose the customer — the payment terms come from their record, so the due date fills itself in. Add a line for each thing you are billing, with a description, a quantity, a unit (metres, hours, lump sum), a rate and a VAT treatment. The totals at the bottom update as you type, broken down by VAT rate.

A draft posts nothing. It is not in the books, it is not on the VAT return, and the customer has not been asked to pay. Save it, check it, come back to it tomorrow.

Stage two: issuing it

The Issue button at the top of the draft

Issuing does three things at once: it posts the transaction to the accounts, it takes the next number in a series that must have no gaps, and it produces a document the customer can reclaim VAT against.

Because of that, an issued invoice cannot be edited. If it is wrong, raise a credit note against it — that leaves both halves on the record, which is what an auditor expects to see.

Printing it for the customer

Print and Download at the top of the invoice produce it as a PDF on your letterhead: titled TAX INVOICE, both TRNs, every line with its quantity, rate, net amount, VAT rate and VAT, the VAT shown rate by rate rather than as one lump — which is what the law requires — and the total in words.

A draft prints too, so it can be checked on paper first, but it carries DRAFT across every page and says under the title that it is not a valid tax invoice. Credit and debit notes print the same way, titled as what they are, and name the invoice they adjust.

Why a reprint never changesThe customer's name, address and TRN are frozen onto the document at the moment you issue it. If they move offices next year, last year's invoice still reads as it was sent.

Credit and debit notes

Choose the document type at the top of a new invoice. A note must name the invoice it adjusts — a note that does not say what it corrects cannot be attributed to anything, and cannot be issued.

Supplier bills

On the Invoices screen, switch to Suppliers billed us. It works the same way with one difference: you enter their invoice number, not one of ours. That is the number an auditor will look for when matching your VAT reclaim to the paperwork.

Reverse charge — buying from abroad

When you buy services from outside the UAE, the supplier charges no VAT and you account for it yourself. Choose Reverse charge as the treatment on those lines. The system then puts the tax on both sides of your books, so it nets to nothing while still being declared in both halves of the VAT return — which is exactly what the FTA requires.

Day book and ledgers

Day Book

Finance → Entry → Day Book

Every voucher in date order — the equivalent of Tally's day book. Filter by voucher type, search by reference, party, narration or an amount. This is where you go when somebody asks "what did we post on the 14th?".

You can also raise a new journal entry from here, which is usually where you notice one is missing.

Ledgers

Finance → Entry → Ledgers

Every movement on one account, with a running balance — or one customer's statement, which you can print and send.

Following a figure backAny total on a report can be traced: click through from the report to the account, from the account to the voucher, and from the voucher to the invoice behind it. Nothing in this system is a number you cannot explain.

Cheques, retention and the bank

Cheque Register (post-dated cheques)

Finance → Registers → Cheques

A large share of UAE business is settled with cheques dated months ahead. The register tracks each one: the number, the bank, the date it can be banked, who holds the paper, and whether it has cleared or bounced. A post-dated cheque you are holding is not cash yet, and the system treats it that way.

Retention

Finance → Registers → Retention

The money a client holds back on each certificate — typically 5 or 10 per cent, half released at handover and half after the defects liability period. The register records what is held, on which job, and the date it becomes releasable, so nothing is quietly forgotten a year later.

It works in both directions: retention a client holds from you, and retention you hold from a subcontractor.

Bank Reconciliation

Finance → Registers → Bank Rec

Tick off each line against the bank statement each month. It is how you prove the cash figure in the accounts is real, rather than assuming it.

Advances

A mobilisation advance is normal on a UAE contract. Ten or fifteen per cent of the value arrives before anybody is on site, and comes back off the certificates a slice at a time over the year that follows. The same happens in the other direction, because a supplier will not order switchgear against a promise.

An advance is not incomeNothing has been supplied when the money moves. Money a customer paid you is owed back until the work is billed. Money you paid a supplier is owed to you until they bill for it. Recording either as income makes one month look wonderful and every month after it look poor.

Advances

Finance → Registers → Advances

Record it when the money moves. Say which way it goes, choose the customer or supplier, and pick the bank account it went through. Either type the amount, or give the contract value and the percentage and let the screen work it out.

Unlike the retention register, this one posts. The money really has moved and nothing else is going to record it, so saving an advance puts it straight into your accounts.

Recovering it

Each time an invoice comes round, click Recover and enter the slice that comes off. What is left updates on its own, and the advance closes itself the moment nothing remains. The screen refuses an amount larger than the balance, because recovering more than was advanced would turn what you owe into an apparent asset.

Recovering an advance moves no money. It reduces what the customer owes, because they have already paid that much. The bank is untouched, because the cash arrived months ago.

VAT on an advance Taking money for taxable work creates a tax point under UAE rules, so a tax invoice is due on it. Raise that on the Invoices screen and then recover the advance against it. No VAT is charged on this screen, which is what stops the same tax being declared twice.

The two other endings

  • Refund when the money is genuinely going back. This moves real cash.
  • Write off when it is not coming back. This takes the balance to the profit and loss, which is a decision rather than a correction.

Job costing

Job Costing

Finance → Job Costing

What each job earned, what it cost, the margin, and how much of its budget is used. Jobs can be nested — a main contract with packages and variations beneath it — and the parent shows a true total without anything being posted against it directly.

Getting labour onto a job

Hours recorded on Attendance against a job are priced at each person's cost rate and charged to that job. Until that happens the margin is missing them, and the screen says so in as many words. The Post labour button does it.

Time booked to no job disappearsHours recorded without a job are not counted against any job's cost. The Manhours report shows them separately so you can see how much is drifting.

Work in progress

Job Costing tells you what a job earned and cost in a period. This answers a different question, and a cumulative one. How far through is each contract, and has the invoicing kept pace with the work?

Work in Progress

Finance → Job Costing → Work in Progress

How far through a contract is, is measured by cost. What has been spent, against what the job was priced to cost. It uses figures already in your ledger and does not depend on anybody’s opinion of how far along the site looks.

Once you know a contract is sixty per cent complete, you know it has earned sixty per cent of its value. Comparing that with what you have actually invoiced is the whole point of the screen.

Billed ahead, and billed behind

On a long contract these two figures are almost never equal, and both are invisible in a profit and loss that simply totals what was invoiced.

What it saysWhat it means
Billed ahead of the workYou have invoiced more than you have earned. That surplus is not profit. It is money held against work you still owe.
Done, not yet billedYou have earned more than you have invoiced. Work is finished and nobody has asked the client to pay for it.
In lineInvoicing has kept pace with the work.
Budget not setNothing about this contract can be measured until a budget cost is on the job.

The two are shown separately and never netted off. One contract a million ahead and another a million behind is not the same business as two sitting exactly on plan.

A contract heading for a loss When a job is expected to cost more than it will ever be paid, the whole loss belongs in this period rather than spread over the months remaining. Those contracts are listed first, under an amber banner. A loss you see in month three is still a decision you can act on. One that surfaces in month eleven is only an explanation.
Why there is no date filterA contract question is cumulative from the day the job started. Filtering to a financial year would report a two-year contract as barely begun every January.

VAT and corporate tax

VAT Return (VAT 201)

Finance → Tax → VAT

The return laid out box by box exactly as the FTA form asks for it: standard-rated supplies, zero-rated, exempt, reverse charge, and the tax on each. It is built from the treatment you set on every line, which is why a single invoice can be split across three boxes.

Before filing, the screen agrees the return to the two VAT control accounts in your books. If they disagree, something was posted to the wrong account and it says so.

Choosing the VAT treatment

TreatmentUse it when
StandardNormal 5% work billed inside the UAE. Most of what you do.
Zero-rated0% but still a taxable supply — exports outside the GCC, international transport.
ExemptNo VAT and not recoverable — certain financial services, bare land, local passenger transport.
Out of scopeOutside UAE VAT altogether — supplies made and consumed abroad.
Reverse chargeYou account for the tax yourself on an import. It goes on both sides and nets to nil.

Corporate Tax

Finance → Tax → Corporate Tax

Nine per cent on taxable profit above AED 375,000, with the return and the payment both due nine months after the period ends. The screen works out the deadline, tracks whether you qualify for Small Business Relief, and carries losses forward within the 75% relief cap.

It also shows why taxable profit is not the same as the profit in your accounts, line by line, so the adjustment is explainable rather than magic.

Closing a period

Once a VAT return is filed, lock the period: Finance → Setup → Lock the books. The Finance Controller can do this, as can a Director on the company record. Nothing can then be posted on or before that date, so the figures behind a return you have already sent cannot move.

Electronic invoicing

UAE eInvoicing does not work by sending anything to the FTA yourself. It runs through an Accredited Service Provider: this system hands them the document, and they validate it, sign it and transmit it. There are five parties involved — you, your provider, your customer's provider, your customer, and the FTA.

Electronic Invoicing

Finance → Tax → e-Invoicing

Every issued invoice with where it has got to. Sent, Accepted and Delivered are separate states rather than one tick, because a document can be accepted by your provider and refused by your customer's hours later — and somebody has to be able to see which happened.

Preview before you send. It shows the exact document that would leave, and lists anything a validator would refuse. A rejection from somebody else's system arrives long after your customer already has the invoice.

Switching it onNothing is transmitted until you name your provider on Finance → Setup, along with the two identifiers they give you. Until then your invoicing is entirely unaffected.
People

Employee records

Employees

HR → People → Employees

Everyone the group employs. Search by name, employee number, mobile, email, department, or any number from an Emirates ID, passport, visa or MOL Person ID.

The profile holds the personal details, the UAE documents with their expiry dates, the contract, the salary and bank details for WPS, and any custom fields your administrator has added.

Supplied labour

Mark a worker as Supplied and name the manpower supplier. They are then kept out of your payroll, out of the WPS file, and out of your workforce mix — because they are sponsored by the supplier and sit on the supplier's establishment, not yours.

The system refuses a duplicate personIf an Emirates ID or passport number is already on somebody else's record, it says whose. This is deliberate: the same man on two records means two salaries and a WPS file the bank rejects.

Onboarding

HR → People → Onboarding

Requisition to offer to joining, so a new hire arrives with their file already complete rather than being assembled afterwards.

Certifications

HR → People → Certifications

Trade tickets, medicals and third-party certificates with their renewal dates.

Attendance and leave

Attendance & Muster

HR → Time → Attendance

The daily muster: who was present, absent, on leave or off. Record the actual in and out times where you have them, because two rules need them — the night overtime rate, and the summer midday ban.

Log the hours against a job here as well. That is what puts labour into job costing.

If you have a punch machine, its file can be imported rather than typed.

Leave

HR → Time → Leave

Requests, approvals and balances. Annual leave builds up from the join date — two days a month between six months and a year, then the full entitlement — so the balance is earned rather than granted, and it is calculated rather than typed.

The summer midday break

Between 15 June and 15 September, outdoor work is banned between 12:30 and 15:00. The Compliance report reads your attendance records and lists any day where somebody appears to have worked through it. The punch data is the evidence either way.

Payroll and WPS

Payroll

HR → Payroll

HR runs the month and reviews every payslip; the Finance Controller (or a Director) then approves the run and marks it paid. HR cannot approve its own run, so the salary run is always checked by a second person. The system works out basic and allowances, overtime at both rates, absence and unpaid-leave deductions, salary-advance recovery, and part months for anyone who joined or left partway through.

Nothing is a guess: every figure traces back to attendance, to the employee's contract, or to your HR policy.

Payslips

Above a run, Print or Download all payslips produces one PDF with each employee on a page of their own, ready to hand out; Payslip on a row prints just that person. Each shows earnings with the days behind a part month, overtime hours, unpaid days, other deductions, advance recovery, and net pay in words. The bank account is printed as its last four characters only. Until the run is approved every page says DRAFT.

Payroll reaches the accounts by itselfMarking a run paid posts the wage cost to the ledger. You do not raise a journal entry for salaries.

"Somebody is not ready"

Payroll will hold a person back rather than pay them wrongly — no salary set, no join date, missing bank details. It names each one and why. Fix the record and run again.

The WPS file for the bank

The SIF file the UAE Wage Protection System requires. The system validates the IBAN with its checksum, the MOL Person ID and the routing code before generating it, and tells you exactly which employee is holding it up — far better than the bank rejecting the whole file.

Overtime, the two rates

Ordinary overtime is 125% of basic. Night work between 22:00 and 04:00, rest days and public holidays are 150%. The hours come from attendance, not from a typed figure, and the Overtime report shows what it is costing and whether anybody has gone over the legal cap of two hours a day.

When someone leaves

Separation & Settlement

HR → People → Separation

The full end-of-service calculation: gratuity at 21 days of basic per year for the first five years and 30 days after, capped at two years' pay; unused leave encashed; pending salary; notice pay; the repatriation ticket. Unpaid leave is excluded from the service period, as the law requires.

The Statement button prints it as a PDF for the employee to sign, with the basis of the gratuity, the employee’s declaration and signature boxes. Until the settlement is approved it says DRAFT across the page — nobody should sign away their claims against a figure nobody has signed off.

Your HR policy

HR Policy

HR → HR Policy

Your handbook, expressed as the numbers every other screen reads. Leave days, when leave starts accruing, carry-forward, probation and notice periods, working hours, overtime rates and caps, sick pay bands, gratuity, and the air ticket.

Set it once per company, and every calculation follows it — leave balances, payslips, overtime, the final settlement.

"That is below what the law allows"The system refuses anything under the statutory minimum and tells you which article of Federal Decree-Law 33/2021 says so. You may always be more generous than the law; you may never be less.
Winning work

The pipeline and site visits

Every price a customer asks for starts here, as an enquiry. The Pipeline is a board with one column for each stage the work moves through, so anybody can see in a glance what is being chased, how far each has got, and what it is really worth.

Pipeline

CRM & Estimation → Pipeline

Click Log an enquiry when a customer asks for a price. Only the title and who it is from are needed at first — the value, the contact and where it came from can follow. Open any card to see where it has got to on a bar like a parcel tracker, and use Move it on as it progresses. The dialog shows what the move does to the chance of winning before you commit to it.

The five lamps on each card count what is known about the enquiry: the budget, the decision-maker, the date it is needed, whether the site has been visited, and whether the scope is defined. Few lamps lit is not a bad enquiry. It is one nobody has done the work on yet, which is something you can act on.

What the pipeline is really worth

A million-dirham enquiry that arrived this morning is not a million dirhams. Each stage carries a chance of winning, and the figure at the top is the weighted total — each enquiry's value multiplied by its chance. The gross total is shown beside it for comparison, but the weighted one is the number that is true.

StageChance counted
New5%
Qualifying10%
Site visit20%
Estimating30%
Quoted40%
Negotiating70%
Won · Lostnot in the forecast

Won work belongs on a job, and lost work is nothing, so neither stays in the forecast.

Losing asks whyMoving an enquiry to Lost will not go through without the reason, and asks who won it. That is the only moment anybody still knows, and it is what Where the Work Comes From is built from.

Site visits and the report

Open the enquiry → Site visits

The visit and the report are two events. Click Record to book the visit with its date and who is going — leave the findings empty if it has not happened yet. After the visit, click File the report and write what was found. Only then does the enquiry show Site Report Submitted.

An empty report is refused: it would mark the enquiry as reported when nothing was, which is worse than no report at all.

Estimates

The price on a quotation is built up from what the work costs, never typed. That is the whole point of an estimate: when somebody asks in six months why a job was priced as it was, the answer is in front of them, line by line.

Estimates

CRM & Estimation → Estimates

Click Start an estimate and set the overhead and the margin straight away — they are the two things nobody goes back to set. Then add each bill-of-quantities item, choosing how it is measured: tonne, metre, piece or lump sum. A lump sum does not ask for a quantity, because the customer is buying an outcome, not a measurement.

For each line, fill in the build-up — material, labour hours and rate, plant hours and rate, subcontract — and the unit cost adds itself up as you type.

Taking material off, with wastage

For anything worth measuring, click Add material on the line: how much one unit needs, and the wastage. The takeoff then becomes that line's material cost. Wastage is asked for on each material rather than once at the end, because it differs — five per cent on a cable drum is a rounding error; five per cent on structural steel is a week of somebody's wages.

Margin is not markup

A twenty per cent markup on cost gives a 16.7 per cent margin on the price. They are different numbers, and quoting one while meaning the other is how a job priced at "twenty per cent" makes less than expected. The Overheads and margin panel shows both side by side before you commit. Overheads go on before the margin, not after.

Marking it pricedMark it priced refuses an estimate with nothing costed, and one that quotes below cost unless somebody has deliberately marked it as a loss leader. Neither should reach a customer by accident.

Quotations

A quotation is a commitment to a price, so three things are true of it: its price comes from the estimate, nothing reaches the customer before management has signed it, and once it has gone out it is revised rather than edited.

Raising and approving

CRM & Estimation → Quotations

Click Raise a quotation and pick the priced estimate — the total follows from it, and there is no box to type a different one. Click Send for approval. It goes to the Operations Manager, then a Director, and to the Managing Director as well when it is AED 500,000 or more. The estimator cannot approve their own work.

Sending it

The approved quotation → Email it to the customer

Tick who it goes to from the customer's contacts and send. The quotation goes as a PDF on your letterhead, attached to the email, from the mailbox set up in Settings → Email. If no mail server has been set up the button reads Issue to the customer: download the PDF, send it from your own mailbox, and record where it went.

The PDF shows the customer, each line with its quantity, unit, rate and amount, the total in words, and your standard terms. It never shows cost, margin or markup — which is exactly why printing the screen is not the same thing.

Revisions

An issued quotation → Raise a revision

The customer is holding a piece of paper, so an issued quotation is never edited. A revision is raised instead, goes through approval again, and prints as Quotation — Revision 2. The old one is kept, marked Superseded, and prints with SUPERSEDED across it, because the conversation will still refer to it.

When the customer orders

An issued quotation → They ordered it

Enter their purchase order number, date and value. If their value differs from the quotation, the difference is shown while you type and has to be confirmed. The job is then created at their figure, with a budget in money and hours taken from the estimate. Customers round, trim scope and agree discounts on the phone; taking the quoted number because it is already in the system makes every margin report on that contract wrong from the first day.

The hit rate is by valueThe quotations list reports how much of what you quote you win by value, not by count. Winning three small quotations and losing the large one is not a good quarter, and a rate by count would report it as one. With fewer than three decided it says "too few to call".
Buying and stores

Items, stores and bins

Purchasing and stores follow one chain, and each step exists because the next cannot be trusted without it: site asks for material, procurement gets prices and orders it, the store receives it, QA/QC passes it, and the store issues it to the job — which is the moment it becomes that contract's cost.

Items

Inventory & SCM → Items

What you buy and keep, each with a short code people will actually type, like CBL-4C-16, and the unit it is measured in — the same unit list invoices use, so a metre bought and a metre billed mean the same thing. Untick Keep stock of this for hire and services. Tick needs QA/QC inspection for anything that must be passed before use.

Standard cost is for estimating only. Stock is always valued at what was actually paid.

Stores, zones and bins

Inventory & SCM → Stores

A store says the company owns 400 metres of cable. A bin says which rack to walk to. Give each store its kind — main store, site store, plant yard or lay-down area — and add bins where the store is divided, with a zone if it has them.

Once a store has bins, receiving into it or issuing from it asks which bin, and shows how much each bin holds. A bin that has ever held anything cannot be deleted; untick still in use so the history stays readable.

Stock on Hand

Inventory & SCM → Stock on Hand

What is on the shelf right now, and what it is worth at average cost. Anything at or below its reorder level is highlighted. A negative balance is never normal: it means something was issued that was never received, so count the shelf and correct it before trusting any other figure.

Material requests

A material request is how site asks for material without committing any money. It names the job, the store, when it is needed and what — and the moment it is raised it goes for approval.

Material Requests

Inventory & SCM → Material Requests

Click Raise a request, choose the job, the store it should be delivered to and the date it is needed on site, add a line for each item and quantity, and click Send for approval. It goes to a Site Engineer, then the Project Manager, then Procurement. Procurement sits last because it acts on the request rather than outranking it.

Each request is checked against what is already on the shelf, and the card says in a sentence what can be issued now and what has to be bought. Once approved, procurement issues what is in stock, raises an enquiry for prices, or clicks Raise an order to turn it straight into a purchase order with the lines filled in.

Say when, not just whatA request with no date is read as whenever, and material that arrives the day after the pour is material that was not ordered.

Enquiries and three quotes

Enquiries & Quotes

Inventory & SCM → Enquiries & Quotes

Click Raise an enquiry and say what is needed and how much — there is no price box, on purpose. Click Ask suppliers and tick at least three. Each appears as a column on the comparison sheet whether or not they reply.

Print the enquiry for each supplier from under their name, or a blank copy from the top for a supplier not yet on file. It carries quantities only, with space for their price, delivery charge, delivery time and how long the price holds — never another supplier's price or what you expect to pay.

As each quotation comes in, click Enter price in that supplier's column. Read across a row to compare one line, or down a column to compare a supplier. The cheapest figure on each line is marked green, which is not always the supplier with the cheapest total.

Awarding

Click Award and choose the winner. It is refused until three suppliers have been asked — with fewer there is nothing to compare the price against. If the winner is not the cheapest you are asked why, and the extra cost is shown while you type; a year from now that note is the only record of the decision. A draft purchase order is then raised at the winner's prices, and the enquiry prints as CLOSED.

A supplier who never repliedkeeps their column, greyed. Leaving them off would make a comparison of two quotes look like a comparison of two suppliers, and their silence is worth seeing.

Purchase orders

The purchase order is the moment money is committed, so it is approved before it counts, and nothing can be received against it until then.

Purchase Orders

Inventory & SCM → Purchase Orders

Click Raise an order, or arrive from an approved request or an awarded enquiry with the lines already in. Choose the supplier, the job, the store to deliver to and the promised date — it is what late deliveries and the supplier's on-time record are measured against. Click Send for approval: the Project Manager, the Operations Manager and a Director, with the Managing Director added from AED 50,000.

Once approved, click Print or Download at the bottom of the card and send the PDF to the supplier. It shows the supplier and their contact, where to deliver, each line with unit price and amount, the order total excluding VAT in words, your purchase order terms, and who raised and approved it.

Receiving against the order

The approved order → Record delivery on a line

Enter how much actually arrived, into which store and bin, and the supplier's delivery note number. Receive what came, not what was ordered: short deliveries are normal, and the order keeps the balance open by itself. How much has arrived is added up from the deliveries rather than typed, so the order moves itself to Partly received and then Received.

No VAT on an orderAn order posts nothing to the accounts and carries no VAT. It is a commitment to buy; the tax point is the supplier's invoice.

Buying what never reaches the store

Some of what you buy has no delivery note and nothing to put on a shelf: PRO work, equipment hire, subcontract labour, consultancy, camp catering. It is ordered like anything else, and closed differently.

  1. Set the service up as an item once, with Keep stock of this unticked — for example “PRO — new employment visa”, “PRO — visa renewal”, “Crane hire, 50t”. Unticked means it never appears on a stock report pretending to be on a shelf.
  2. Raise the purchase order against the supplier, one line per service. It goes through the same approval route as material: Project Manager, Operations Manager, Director, and the Managing Director above AED 50,000.
  3. When the work is done, click Mark work as delivered on the order. There is nothing to receive into a store, so this is what closes it, and the order records who confirmed it and on what date. That sentence is the evidence the bill is due, which is why it is a named person rather than a tick.
  4. Record the supplier’s bill under Finance → Invoices as a Purchase invoice, and choose the order under Against purchase order. The order then lists what has been billed against it.

Where the cost lands. PRO work, visas and recruitment are not a customer’s job — they are what the company carries itself — so charge those lines to the Administration & HR cost centre rather than to a job. Each invoice line is charged to one or the other, never both: the same cost on a job report and an overhead report would be counted twice. Hire and subcontract labour for a particular contract do belong to that job.

VAT on a PRO invoiceThese usually mix two things: the PRO company’s own service fee, which carries 5% VAT, and government fees they paid on your behalf, which are outside VAT. Put them on separate lines and set the second line’s treatment to Out of scope. One line at 5% over the whole invoice claims input tax you are not entitled to.

An order with even one stock item on it cannot be closed this way. The material still has to be received into a store, because that is what puts it on the shelf and into the accounts.

Receiving, inspecting and issuing

Receive & Issue

Inventory & SCM → Receive & Issue → Record a movement

Say what happened and the form asks only for what that kind needs. Material arrived: the quantity, what one unit cost, and the delivery note. Material out to a job: the quantity and the job — the price comes from the shelf, not from you. Moved between our stores: the second store, and the bin it goes into.

Nothing here is edited afterwards. A correction is a new movement, so the history still explains itself a year later.

What each movement does to the accounts

MovementWhat it does
Delivery receivedBecomes stock, valued at what was paid. Not a cost on any job yet.
Issued to a jobLeaves the shelf at average cost and becomes that job's material cost.

QA/QC inspection

Receive & Issue → Inspect, on a delivery showing Pending

A delivery of an item that needs inspection arrives on the shelf as Pending, and cannot be issued until somebody has looked at it. Pass or fail it and record the certificate number. Passed material is free to issue straight away. Failed material stays on the shelf and in stock value — it is still yours until it physically goes back — and cannot be issued.

If someone tries to issue more than is free, the refusal says how much is waiting on inspection or rejected, so nobody stares at a full shelf wondering why the system says there is none. An inspection is recorded once and cannot be changed afterwards.

Every delivery, issue, transfer and return to a supplier has a printable note — see Printed documents.

Returns from site

Returns from Site

Inventory & SCM → Returns from Site → Record a return

Choose the job it is coming back from, the store it goes into, and who handed it over. Add a line for each item and set each to Reusable or Scrap. The sentence at the bottom says, before you save, how much goes back on the shelf and what the job is credited.

The form shows what the job still has out, and refuses more than that — returning material a job never had would quietly improve its margin for no reason anybody could later find.

Split mixed linesSixty metres reusable and forty of offcuts on the same note is normal. Enter them as two lines rather than rounding to whichever is bigger: reusable credits the job, scrap keeps its cost on the job because the job caused it.

Equipment and calibration

Equipment & Calibration

Inventory & SCM → Equipment & Calibration

Stock is quantities; this register is individual tools with serial numbers, and the question asked of each is whether this one can still be trusted. Tick the certificate box for anything that needs calibrating — a torque wrench does, a shovel does not — and click Calibrate when it comes back from the lab, with the date, the result and the certificate number.

The expiry is worked out from the calibration date, not from today, so a postal delay does not quietly extend it.

Blocked from useA tool out of calibration, never calibrated, away for calibration, under repair or withdrawn cannot be put on a job. A reading taken with an instrument whose certificate lapsed last month is not a reading, it is a number.

Supplier performance

Supplier Performance

Inventory & SCM → Supplier Performance

How each supplier has actually performed — on time and in full, rejections at inspection, replies to enquiries, and price — read back out of what happened, never typed in. The small grey number under each figure is how many orders, inspections or enquiries it is out of, and where there are too few it says so instead of showing a percentage.

The same figures appear beside the prices on each enquiry's comparison sheet, so past performance is in front of you at the moment you choose.

Making the figures workPut a promised date on purchase orders and record inspections. An order with no promised date is left out rather than counted as on time, and an order still running is not late until its date has passed.

Your letterhead and templates

Printed Documents

Settings → Printed Documents

One screen per company decides how every document looks. Out of the box the system draws a letterhead from the company record: logo, name, address and TRN, with a coloured rule in your accent colour. When your designer produces a proper letterhead, upload the top band as Header artwork and it replaces the generated one on every page of every document at once — nobody waits for a new release. Also set here: the phone, email and website under the name; a footer line such as the trade licence number; whether signature boxes print; and the standard terms printed on quotations, purchase orders and enquiries. A quotation with terms of its own uses those instead.

Click Print next to See it on paper to check the saved settings on an invented document before a customer sees a real one.

PNG or JPEG onlyPDFs cannot draw SVG, WebP or GIF images, so artwork in those formats is refused, and a company logo in one of them is left off with a yellow warning. About 2000 pixels wide is plenty for a band across an A4 page. Check the sample in print preview: artwork that looks fine on screen is often too tall once it repeats across the top of every page.

Each company has its own settings, so a WBTS quotation never goes out on WBE paper. A template that changes the layout of a document, rather than its letterhead, is a change the project team makes. Arabic text is not yet supported on the PDFs.

Two reports worth a closer look

Cash Flow Forecast

Finance → Reports → Cash Flow

Every other finance report looks backwards. This one looks forward: what is in the bank today, then thirteen weeks of everything already dated to move — cheques you hold and cheques you have written, invoices on their due dates, retention on its release date, and wages on payday.

The sentence at the top says in one line whether you are fine, getting tight, or heading for an overdraft — and in which week. Any week that closes below zero is shaded red.

Turn on Cautious view before promising anybody anything. It removes money you are owed but cannot be sure of, while keeping every payment you must make. If the answer is still yes, it is a real yes.

Workforce Mix

HR → Reports → Workforce Mix

The nationality mix of your own employees, measured against the share you are working to. Where you are over, it gives three ways to close the gap: hires needed, the equivalent reduction, and how many of those visas fall due within ninety days.

Supplied labour is excluded — those workers sit on the supplier's establishment, not yours.

Running the system

Approvals

Anything that needs a second pair of eyes goes through the approvals inbox. An administrator sets the routes: which kinds of request need approving, and by whom, based on the approval level of each role.

  • Raising one: the request goes to whoever the route names, and they are notified.
  • Acting on one: open Approvals, read it, approve or reject with a reason. The reason is kept.
  • The bell in the top bar shows how many things are waiting on you.
  • You see only the requests that concern you: ones you raised, ones you decided, and ones your role could decide. Directors see them all. My pending approvals lists only what you can decide right now.
  • A step can be decided by the role it names or anyone with a higher approval level. A role below that level does not see the step in its queue.
  • Four eyes. Nobody approves a request they raised, and nobody decides two steps of the same request — a senior person may act for a junior step, but then the next step needs someone else. The screen says so if you try.

Email for quotations

Email

Settings → Email

Nothing about your mail server is written into the system. The server, port, security and mailbox are set here, per company, so each company sends as itself. Choosing Microsoft 365 fills in the server, port and security. The settings that catch people out are the server (smtp.office365.com, not outlook.com) and the port (587 with STARTTLS). Microsoft switches SMTP sign-in off on newer tenants, so IT may need to enable it for the mailbox, and an app password is needed where multi-factor sign-in is on.

Save, then Send a test — it goes to you, not to a customer. If it fails, the message underneath is what your mail server actually said, which is the line to give IT. The password is encrypted and never shown back; leaving the box empty when you change something else keeps the saved one.

Every quotation sent is listed at the bottom of the screen. The result says Accepted rather than Delivered, because whether the customer received it, read it or found it in junk is beyond anything a sending system can know.

Users and access

Access is granted per screen, and separately for viewing, creating, editing, deleting and approving. A site storekeeper might see only Inventory; an accountant sees Finance. If a screen is not granted, it is not in the menu at all — and typing its address simply returns you to your dashboard.

Whoever orders does not receive, and whoever receives does not pass the inspection. HR prepares the payroll run and the Finance Controller approves it. How long records are kept

Two different things, often confused, so they are separated here.

Your business records are kept for as long as the system exists. Invoices, journal entries, payroll runs, attendance, settlements, stock movements, quotations: nothing removes them, and no setting anywhere shortens them. They have to stay, because a trial balance, a job cost report and an end-of-service calculation all read years of history — and because UAE law expects accounting records to be kept for several years. Confirm the exact periods with your auditor; the system’s job is to still have them.

Three logs have a period you can set, at Settings → Data Retention, because they grow with every action and nothing reports on them:

WhatKept by defaultThen
Audit trail — who changed what1 yearMoves to the audit archive, still readable on the Audit Log screen
Sent emails — the record that a quotation or order went out3 yearsMoves to the sent-email archive. It is the evidence a document was sent, asked for long afterwards
Notifications — the alerts in the bell menu6 monthsRemoved once read, because each is a copy of something recorded elsewhere. Unread ones are never removed

The housekeeping runs by itself each time the system is updated. The buttons on that page only do it sooner, and the page shows how many records are waiting. Only an administrator can change a period, every change is written to the audit trail, and no period can be set shorter than its floor — 30 days for the audit trail, a year for sent emails.

What this is not forArchiving is about keeping log tables from growing without limit, and about being able to tell an auditor what is kept and for how long. It is not a speed control: the system is not slowed by having last year’s invoices in it.

The audit trail

Audit Log

Administration → Audit Log

A permanent record of who did what and when — created, updated, approved, deleted, posted — together with the address and device it came from. Sign-ins, sign-outs, failed attempts and lock-outs are recorded here too.

Nothing here can be edited or deleted by anybody, including an administrator. Entries older than the period you set move to an Archive tab, which is never emptied, and both tabs show their counts so nothing ever looks lost.

Finding something in it

A year of entries cannot be scrolled, so the trail can be questioned directly. The search box looks at what was done and who did it. More filters adds one person, one kind of action, one kind of record, and a date range. They combine, so everything one person deleted in a single week is three clicks.

  • The sentence above the list always says what you are looking at, so a filtered list is never mistaken for the whole trail.
  • Dates include the whole of the last day. Asking up to the 14th includes everything done on the 14th.
  • Switching between Recent and Archive keeps your filters rather than throwing them away.
Sending somebody what you foundThe address bar carries the filters. Copy the link and send it, and they open exactly the view you were looking at.
Names you do not recogniseA failed sign-in against an unknown email address is ordinary background noise on any site facing the internet. A run of them against one of your real accounts is not.

Glossary

Advance
Money that changes hands before any work is supplied. One received is owed back until the work is billed; one paid is owed to you until the supplier bills for it. Neither is income.
Ageing
Sorting what you are owed by how old it is — not yet due, 1–30 days, 31–60, 61–90, over 90.
Bin
A numbered place inside a store — a rack, shelf or bay — so the record says where material is, not just that you have it.
Corporate Tax (CT)
UAE tax on business profit, 9% above AED 375,000. Separate from VAT, with its own registration number.
Cost centre
A part of your own business you want to measure — a department, a workshop, a site office. Different from a job, which is work you sell.
Credit note
A document that cancels or reduces an invoice already issued.
Defects liability period
The months after handover during which you must return and fix defects. The second half of retention is usually released at the end of it.
DRAFT, NOT APPROVED
Marks printed across a document that is not final. They disappear by themselves once it is approved or issued.
Estimate
The build-up of what a piece of work will cost — material, labour, plant, subcontract — with the overhead and margin on top, from which the quotation’s price comes.
FTA
Federal Tax Authority — VAT and corporate tax.
Gratuity
End-of-service pay, earned after one completed year: 21 days of basic per year for the first five, 30 days a year after that.
GRN (goods received note)
The record of what actually arrived on a delivery, against the purchase order, signed by the store and the driver.
Hit rate
How much of what you quote you win. Measured here by value, not by count.
ILOE
Involuntary Loss of Employment insurance. A small compulsory scheme; the fine for not subscribing is AED 400.
Job
A piece of work you sell — a contract, a project, a service call. Costs and revenue are tagged to it so you can see whether it made money.
Loss leader
A price deliberately set below cost, usually to win a customer. The system only allows it when somebody has said so.
Margin and markup
Markup is added to cost; margin is the share of the price that is profit. A 20% markup is a 16.7% margin.
Material request
Site asking for material for a job. It commits no money and is approved before anything is bought.
MOHRE
Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation — labour law, work permits and establishment classification.
MOL Person ID
The unique number identifying a worker to the Ministry. It goes in the WPS file. Formerly called the labour card number.
Muster
The daily attendance record on site.
Nafis / Emiratisation
The requirement to employ a share of UAE nationals, and the programme supporting it.
Onerous contract
One expected to cost more than it will ever be paid. The whole expected loss is taken now rather than spread over the months remaining.
OTIF
On time in full — a delivery that arrived by the promised date with everything ordered.
PDC
Post-dated cheque — a cheque that cannot be banked until the date written on it.
Purchase order (PO)
The company’s commitment to buy from a supplier, approved before anything can be received against it.
Reverse charge
When you buy from abroad and account for the VAT yourself instead of the supplier charging it.
Retention
Money a client holds back from each payment as security, released in stages.
RFQ (request for quotation)
An enquiry asking several suppliers to price the same material, so the prices can be compared before an order is placed.
Small Business Relief
Relief from corporate tax for businesses under AED 3,000,000 of revenue, available for periods ending on or before 31 December 2029.
Takeoff
Measuring how much material each unit of work needs, with its wastage, so the estimate’s material cost is counted rather than guessed.
TRN
Tax Registration Number — the 15-digit number the FTA issues on VAT registration. It must appear on every tax invoice.
VAT 201
The FTA's VAT return form.
Voucher
One transaction recorded in the accounts, with its own permanent number.
Weighted pipeline
The value of open enquiries, each multiplied by its chance of being won at its stage. Smaller than the gross total, and more honest.
WPS
Wage Protection System. Salaries must be paid through it, using a SIF file sent to your bank.
Work in progress (WIP)
The gap between what a contract has earned and what has been invoiced for it. Billed ahead of the work is a liability; work done and not billed is an asset.
Zero-rated vs exempt
Both charge no VAT, but zero-rated is still a taxable supply and lets you reclaim your input tax. Exempt does not.
