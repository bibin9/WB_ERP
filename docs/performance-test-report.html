WB Group ERP Performance Test

Load, volume and simultaneous-use testing of Pre-Prod, what it found, what was fixed, and the re-test that proves it — 17 September 2026.

Environment
Pre-Prod (Railway)
Build tested after fixes

Test data left behind
None
Result

The test found two ways simultaneous use could corrupt stock and order records, one way a slow save could leave stock without its accounting entry, and bursts of new documents being refused. All four are fixed, deployed to Pre-Prod and re-tested: stock never went negative, no order line was over-received, the ledger stayed balanced, and every test record was removed afterwards.

What was tested

PartWhat it does
VolumeLoads years of history into a scratch company — 20,000 vouchers and 30,000 stock movements at full scale, half that for the re-test — and times the reports and stock screens against it.
Simultaneous useMany people doing exactly the same thing at the same instant: posting vouchers, issuing the last of the stock, recording deliveries against one order line, raising purchase orders and material requests.
Sustained loadA mixed crowd of 10, then 25, users posting, receiving, issuing and running reports continuously for a minute each.
SoundnessAfter all that: the trial balance, duplicate or unbalanced vouchers, negative stock, and stock movements without their accounting entry.
Web server10, 50 and 100 simultaneous visitors to pages that need no sign-in, for 20 seconds each.
Clean-upEvery table in the database counted before the test wrote anything, and again after the scratch company was removed. The run fails if a single count differs.

The database tests ran twice against Pre-Prod before the fixes, then against a PostgreSQL rehearsal database on the test machine, then against Pre-Prod again with the fixes deployed.

What was found, and what was done

IDFindingImpactStatus
PT-01Stock could be issued below zero. Twenty-five people issuing one each from a shelf of ten, at the same moment, left the shelf at minus fourteen.Stock and job costs wrongFixed
PT-02An order line could be received more than once over. Ten deliveries of five recorded at once against a line for ten were all accepted: fifty received.Stock and supplier liability overstatedFixed
PT-03Bursts of new documents were refused. Of 25 purchase orders or material requests raised at once, 19–20 were refused; of 50 vouchers, 24.Work refused, no data damageFixed
PT-04A voucher posting that timed out while waiting its turn raised an error instead of refusing, and left 23 stock movements with no accounting entry. Found by the first re-test.Stock and ledger disagreeFixed
PT-05Two material return notes saved at the same moment took the same number and the second failed with a database error. Found in code review during the fix.Work refused with an unreadable errorFixed
PT-06Stock on Hand and Receive & Issue loaded every stock movement ever recorded to work out balances.Screens slow down every yearFixed
PT-07Two return notes for the same job saved at the same instant could both pass the “still out on the job” check.Rare; job could be over-creditedOpen

How they were fixed

  • PT-01, PT-02: the check and the save are now one step, taken while holding a database lock on that item in that store (and on the order line, for deliveries). A second person waits a moment and then sees the first person's save.
  • PT-03, PT-05: vouchers, purchase orders, material requests, estimates, enquiries, quotations, supplier enquiries and return notes take their next number under a lock for that numbering series, so simultaneous saves queue instead of colliding.
  • PT-04: giving up after waiting too long is now a plain refusal — “The system is busy… try again in a moment.” — and any record saved before its accounting entry is removed if the posting fails for any reason.
  • PT-06: the database adds the movements up per item and store, and the screens read the totals. The figures are identical; a test checks it.

Simultaneous saves, next to the database

From the test machine every Pre-Prod query crosses the internet (about 300 ms each), which stretches how long each save holds its turn. The rehearsal database sits beside the test, the way the live application sits beside its own database, so it shows how queues actually behave.

BurstBefore the fixes · one-tenth volumeAfter the fixes · full volume
50 vouchers posted at once41 of 50 saved50 of 50 · 0.9 s
25 purchase orders raised at once8 of 25 saved25 of 25 · 0.4 s
25 material requests raised at once10 of 25 saved25 of 25 · 0.2 s
25 users, one minute, mixed work82 writes refused0 refused · 4,810 operations
Why Pre-Prod still showed “busy” refusals in bursts Over the 300 ms link, one save holds its turn for about 1.5 seconds, so the back of a queue of 25 or 50 passes the 30-second limit and is refused — cleanly, with nothing half-saved. Beside the database the same queue clears in under a second, as the rehearsal results show. The 15–45 second save times in the Pre-Prod run are the link, not the application.

Checks added so this cannot come back

  • Simultaneous use — fires the same bursts at the shipped code on every test run: the shelf never below zero, no line over-received, every document in a burst numbered with nothing skipped. Removing the lock fails six checks.
  • System busy — holds the locks deliberately until a save gives up: it must refuse politely, write nothing, and leave no stock without its accounting entry. Removing that clean-up fails two checks.
  • The full suite — 4,015 checks across 88 suites — passes on the local database and on PostgreSQL.
